1. Introduction

[PDF_BLOCK b2]
1.1 Purpose

[PDF_BLOCK b3]
The purpose of this document is to assist Cadence in understanding and operationalizing the enhancements made
to its Third-Party Risk Management (TPRM) capabilities through the implementation of the OneTrust TPRM solution.
This engagement aims to provide Cadence with a standardized, scalable, and automated solution that aligns with its
business, operational, and compliance requirements. l

[PDF_BLOCK b4]
As part of this engagement, Optiv supported the implementation and configuration of Cadence’s OneTrust TPRM
instance. This comprises:

[PDF_BLOCK b5]

Designing and implementing structured workflows aligned with Cadence’s operational needs and TPRM
requirements.

[PDF_BLOCK b6]

Configuring user roles to ensure appropriate access management.

[PDF_BLOCK b7]

Establishing vendor onboarding attributes to streamline supplier risk processes.

[PDF_BLOCK b8]

Refreshing and tailoring the Due Diligence Questionnaire to ensure alignment with Cadence’s risk
assessment objectives and regulatory requirements.

[PDF_BLOCK b9]
Through this effort, Cadence will attain a more structured, transparent, and efficient TPRM framework, reducing
manual effort and ensuring compliance with internal and external risk requirements.

[PDF_BLOCK b10]
1.2 Scope

[PDF_BLOCK b11]
The scope of this document focuses on Cadence’s TPRM processes, and the responsibilities of internal
stakeholders involved in managing third-party risks. The following activities are included within this scope:

[PDF_BLOCK b12]

Current State Analysis: Review the current state of Cadence’s OneTrust TPRM instance, including its
configuration, workflows, and supporting processes, while also assessing alignment with the broader TPRM
program.

[PDF_BLOCK b13]

Refreshing the Due Diligence Questionnaire: Enhance and update existing questionnaires to align with
Cadence’s organizational structure, compliance needs, and industry best practices.

[PDF_BLOCK b14]

Configuration of OneTrust TPRM: Document and configure custom workflows, rules, questionnaire
templates, and notification processes in OneTrust tailored to Cadence’s requirements.

[PDF_BLOCK b15]

Training & Knowledge Transfer: Provide documentation covering OneTrust setup and configuration,
workflow logics and questionnaire rules, ensuring Cadence stakeholders can effectively manage and
operate the solution.

3. Transition Toward Integrated Risk Management

[PDF_BLOCK b2]
Based on: OneTrust TPRM Training

[PDF_BLOCK b3]
The visual above, adapted from OneTrust’s framework, illustrates the maturity progression from compliance-driven
and siloed risk functions toward integrated and business-enabling risk management.

[PDF_BLOCK b4]
Currently, Cadence’s TPRM program is positioned at the “Siloed Risk Function” stage, where risk ownership and
assessment activities are distributed across functions such as Legal, Compliance, Security, and Procurement. Each
area maintains its own risk processes, resulting in fragmented oversight.

[PDF_BLOCK b5]
The strategic objective of this engagement is to transition Cadence toward the “Integrated Risk Management (IRM)”
stage - where all third-party risks are collectively managed through a unified, automated, and standardized
framework within OneTrust. This will establish a single source of truth for vendor risk visibility, governance, and
decision-making.

[PDF_BLOCK b6]
A key enabler of this transformation is the integration between Ironclad and OneTrust, which connects contract
lifecycle management with third-party risk workflows. Through this integration:

[PDF_BLOCK b7]

Contract creation and execution in Ironclad automatically triggers corresponding due diligence and
assessment workflows in OneTrust.

[PDF_BLOCK b8]

Vendor-related data (e.g., contract terms, renewal dates, risk tier, and compliance status) synchronizes
between systems for continuous visibility.

[PDF_BLOCK b9]

Risk and contractual obligations are managed cohesively within a single ecosystem.

[PDF_BLOCK b10]
Proprietary and Confidential
7

[PDF_BLOCK b1]
Optiv’s implementation approach supports this evolution by consolidating all risk-related processes into OneTrust
and aligning them with two primary risk domains:

[PDF_BLOCK b2]

Operational Risks:

[PDF_BLOCK b3]
Identified through due diligence questionnaires covering Legal, Financial, Privacy, and Compliance areas.
These assessments capture vendor-level dependencies, regulatory exposures, and process inefficiencies at
both organizational and product/service levels.

[PDF_BLOCK b4]

Cyber Risks:

[PDF_BLOCK b5]
Identified through security questionnaires focusing on data protection, access control, incident
management, and infrastructure security.

[PDF_BLOCK b6]
By structuring risk assessments around these two domains, and integrating Ironclad with OneTrust, Cadence can
now:

[PDF_BLOCK b7]

Manage operational and cybersecurity risks in a unified workstream.

[PDF_BLOCK b8]

Automate due diligence, escalation, and remediation workflows.

[PDF_BLOCK b9]

Maintain cross-functional visibility across all risk stakeholders.

[PDF_BLOCK b10]

Strengthen accountability through consistent, platform-driven governance.

[PDF_BLOCK b11]

Progress toward a mature Integrated Risk Management model that aligns risk oversight with Cadence’s
business objectives and growth strategy.

4.2. Workflow for Third-Party types

[PDF_BLOCK b22]
Cadence’s third-party workflows in OneTrust are now fully automated and tailored to the criticality and type of
vendor.

[PDF_BLOCK b23]
Third parties are classified based on data sensitivity and risk criticality, with separate workflows established for Tier 1,
Tier 2, and Tier 3 vendors. Tier 3 third parties typically represent lower-risk engagements (e.g., limited data access
or short-term service contracts), while Tiers 1 and 2 represent medium- and high-risk third parties requiring
enhanced due diligence.

[PDF_BLOCK b24]
Additionally, classification by third-party Category ensures that distinct workflows are automatically triggered for
Staffing Vendors, Service Providers, Marketing Partners, Revenue Partners, and International Vendors.

[PDF_BLOCK b25]
When a new third-party is added to the OneTrust platform, it is routed through an automated onboarding workflow
that analyzes both third-party tier and category. Based on these attributes, the system assigns the appropriate
workflow path for due diligence, assessment, and approval.

[PDF_BLOCK b26]
4.2.1 Onboarding Workflow

[PDF_BLOCK b27]
Cadence’s OneTrust environment has been configured with automated workflows that manage the lifecycle of third
-party assessments based on both vendor category and risk tier. The integration between Ironclad and OneTrust
ensures that every new vendor entering the system is automatically analyzed and assigned to the appropriate
workflow.

5.1 Final Approved Workflow

[PDF_BLOCK b3]
The following workflow diagram represents the approved configuration that was demonstrated and validated during
the review sessions with the Cadence team.

[PDF_BLOCK b4]
The workflow begins in Ironclad, where a New Vendor Request Form or Contract Submission Form is initiated.
Vendor details from Ironclad are automatically transmitted into OneTrust through integration. Once received, the
vendor attributes are used to determine the appropriate workflow path and initiate the due diligence process.

[PDF_BLOCK b5]
The OneTrust configuration includes automated routing for vendor onboarding, assessment, renewal, and
offboarding, driven by predefined rules and risk tier classification.

[PDF_BLOCK b6]
This workflow design ensures:

[PDF_BLOCK b7]

Consistent automation of vendor onboarding and assessment processes.
